# HAHA — Narrative & Video Script

> **HAHA — Humans Are Hard to Automate**
> Tell a joke that fools the AI and get paid.

This document contains the official narrative of HAHA in a professional and minimalist style: from the problem, why HAHA exists, to the endgame. The narrative text is written in English for a global audience.

---

## 1. Manifesto (Core Narrative)

**The problem**

Artificial intelligence has read almost everything humanity ever wrote.
It can pass exams, write code, and summarize a library in seconds.

But the most human things were never written down.

The pause before someone says *"I'm fine."*
The reason a joke lands in one room and dies in another.
The difference between *"sure"* and *"sure."*
What everyone at a dinner table knows, but no one says.

Machines have learned our words.
They have not learned what we mean.

This is not a small gap. It is the gap between an assistant and a companion, between a tool that answers and a system that understands. And the data needed to close it is rare, expensive, and almost impossible to manufacture. It lives only in people.

**Why HAHA**

HAHA turns that gap into a game.

Anyone can take the stage and tell the AI something only a human would get: a joke, a line with a double meaning, a message with something unsaid, a moment from real life that doesn't follow the rules.

If the AI misses it, and a crowd of real people agrees, you get paid.

Every attempt, won or lost, goes into the Joke Vault: an open, growing record of human nuance, verified by humans. Every miss teaches our AI, the Straight Man, something it didn't know. Each season, it gets harder to fool.

The value is shared. Trading fees fund the humans who beat the machine. The data is licensed to those building the next generation of AI, and the people who created it are part of the story, not raw material for it.

**The endgame**

HAHA's goal is not to prove that AI is foolish.
It is to make AI less foolish, with people, not around them.

We are building the world's largest verified record of what it means to be human: humor, feeling, context, and common sense. A dataset no lab can scrape, because it has to be earned.

And one day, the Straight Man will finally get the joke.

On that day, the people who taught it will have been paid for it.

**Humans are hard to automate. That's the point.**

## 3. Visual Direction (Video Style Guide)

**Mood:** Calm, honest, warm, slightly melancholic at first, then light and hopeful. Not crypto hype, not neon futuristic.

**Visual Style**
- **Documentary, real footage.** Handheld camera, natural lighting, fine grain. Faces of regular people from diverse cultures and ages: markets, trains, kitchens, offices, city streets at night.
- **Small human moments.** A glance before laughing, a polite smile, two close friends laughing without words, an older person reading a text and pausing thoughtfully.
- **Robot as "The Straight Man".** A deadpan humanoid robot character observing humans: sitting in a comedy club audience, watching people laugh in a park, staring at a screen displaying "I'm fine." It tries to understand, but remains slightly puzzled.
- **Minimal typography.** White text over dark background or over footage, one sentence per scene, clean sans-serif font, generous negative space.
- **Color Palette:** Warm and neutral (deep black, bone white, warm stage amber). Accented solely with the HAHA gold brand color.

**Audio**
- Minimalist piano or ambient soundscape. Emphasize pauses and silence as structural elements.
- Diegetic audio from footage (laughter, city ambiance) slowly fading in.
- Voice-over: Calm, mature, measured tone. Or text-only on screen without VO.

**What to Avoid**
- Price charts, candlesticks, rockets, "to the moon", or profit promises.
- Referencing or showing other commercial AI logos.
- Recognizable real public figures without explicit release agreements.
- Creepy or malicious depictions of AI. The Straight Man must feel innocent, curious, and sympathetic.

## 6. Production Notes

- **Footage:** Blend of licensed documentary stock footage (real world moments) and custom production shots for the comedy club environment.
- **Straight Man:** Can be rendered via 3D/CGI, a custom practical animatronic, or an actor with minimalist prosthetic/cyber makeup. Design must remain original and distinct.
- **Talent:** Standard commercial releases for any identifiable talent.
- **Localization:** Subtitled in English and Mandarin for primary international markets.
- **Compliance:** Avoid speculative tokens or investment terminology. Payouts must simply be framed as "get paid" / "rewards in USDG".
- **Call-to-Action:** "Take the stage at [domain]. Rewards vary based on Tip Jar balance. Not financial advice."
